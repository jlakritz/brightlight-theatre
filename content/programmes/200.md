    ## Two hundred years is a very long time.


    Long enough for generations to be born, grow old and disappear. Long enough
    for buildings to change, traditions to fade, new ones to take their place,
    and for things that once seemed permanent to become memories.


    But what does a place actually remember?


    That was the question that started 200.


    I wanted to tell a story about what is passed down: the things we inherit,
    the things we change, and the things that somehow remain. I wanted to be
    inspired by the history of St Peter's, without simply retelling it. Instead,
    the history became a starting point for stories about the people who have
    lived alongside this building — about love and loss, progress and change,
    belonging and community.


    And 200 has very much been a story about our community in its making, too.
    The play has grown through conversations with Brightlight, with Jeremy and
    Alison contributing their own writing and ideas, and with the cast and
    company helping to shape the stories and themes along the way. I wanted to
    include local voices and experiences, and to ask what it is about a place
    like this that allows it to remain a centre of a community even as almost
    everything around it changes.


    Mary Bennett's wonderful histories of St Peter's were an important source of
    inspiration for several of the stories and plotlines. They provided the
    factual threads from which we could begin to weave something new.


    The result is a play that isn't really about the history of a building. It's
    about the people who pass through it — and what they leave behind.


    I hope that whether you have been part of St Peter's for generations, have
    only recently discovered it, or have no particular connection with the
    church at all, there is something here that feels familiar. Because although
    200 begins with a very particular place, the things we inherit, the things
    we lose, and the stories we pass on belong to all of us.


    Finally, a huge thank you to everyone who has brought 200 to life. To the
    cast for their generosity, imagination and commitment; to the crew for
    everything they have done behind the scenes; and to everyone in Brightlight
    who has contributed ideas, challenged things, encouraged us and helped the
    play become what it is. A play like this may have one writer's name on it,
    but this one genuinely belongs to a lot of people.


    Thank you for being part of its story.
  directors:
    - Jamie Lakritz
