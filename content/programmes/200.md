    ## Two hundred years is a very long time.


    Long enough for generations to be born, grow old and disappear. Long enough
    for buildings to change, traditions to fade, new ones to take their place,
    and for things that once seemed permanent to become stories and memories.


    But what does a place actually remember?


    That was the question that started **200**.


    When the opportunity arose to create a brand new piece of theatre to
    celebrate the bicentenary of St Peter’s Church, I knew I didn’t want to
    simply tell the story of the building. Two hundred years of history is
    fascinating, but a list of dates and events isn’t the most theatrical of
    stories. I was excited to explore what might happen if we thought about that
    history through the people who had lived alongside it and through the things
    that a place might somehow remember. Instead, we have used the history as a
    starting point, the spark of a flame, to explore the universal human themes
    they revealed. 


    And so the story we will tell is a story about what is passed down. The
    things we inherit, the things we change, and the things that somehow remain.
    Stories about love and loss, progress and change, belonging and community.


    And 200 has very much been a story about our community in its making, too.
    Our first month of rehearsal was dedicated to exploring the history and
    finding new ways to retell it. The plot lines you'll see tonight grew from
    those first workshops. Over the next month, the script started to come
    together, as Jeremy and Alison Lake and I plotted out the stories and wrote
    the dialogue, with the cast and company continuing to shape the stories and
    themes along the way. It was important we allowed local voices and
    experiences to find their way into the piece, and to ask what it is about a
    place like this that allows it to remain a centre of a community even as
    almost everything around it changes. A big thank you to everyone who
    contributed their memories or birthday wishes. You helped shape this play
    more than you could know.


    Mary Bennett’s wonderful histories of St Peter’s were another important
    source of inspiration for several of the stories and plotlines. They
    provided the factual threads from which we could begin to weave something
    new, taking real people, events and details and allowing them to spark
    stories that could belong to any generation.


    The result is a play that isn’t really about the history of a building. It’s
    about the people who pass through it, the marks they leave, and the stories
    that remain.


    I hope that whether you have been part of St Peter’s for generations, have
    only recently discovered it, or have no particular connection with the
    church at all, there is something here that feels familiar. Because although
    200 begins with a very particular place, the things we inherit, the things
    we lose, and the stories we pass on belong to all of us.


    Finally, a huge thank you to everyone who has brought 200 to life. To the
    cast for their generosity, imagination and commitment; to the crew for
    everything they have done behind the scenes; and to everyone in Brightlight
    who has contributed ideas, challenged things, encouraged us and helped the
    play become what it is - our most ambitious new work to date.


    **For 200 years, people have come and gone. Their stories remain.**


    I hope you enjoy the show. 
  directors:
    - Jamie Lakritz
